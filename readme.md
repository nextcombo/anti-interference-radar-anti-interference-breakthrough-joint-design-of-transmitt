In today's widespread application of radar technology, the game between jamming and anti-jamming remains one of the core challenges. Intermittent Sampling Forward Jamming (ISRJ) as a new radar coherent jamming technique based on undersampling principles, with its real-time and rapid response capability, can generate dense pseudo targets that traditional anti-jamming technologies find difficult to counteract. The team from National University of Defense Technology, Zhou Kai, proposed a "Joint Design of Radar Transmit Waveform and Non-Matched Filtering" method, providing an innovative approach to solve this problem, and related research findings have been published in the "Journal of Electronics and Information" for the year 2021, Issue 7.

Research background: The "difficulty" of ISRJ interference and the limitations of existing technology
1. Core threat of ISRJ interference
ISRJ interference is based on digital radio frequency storage technology, which achieves "time-division reception and transmission, alternating sampling and forwarding", ultimately forming dense pseudo target interference. This type of interference can precisely utilize the radar matched filtering characteristics, even low sidelobe signals are difficult to resist, greatly interfering with radar target detection.
2. Limitations of current anti-ISRJ technologies
Currently, there are mainly two types of methods to combat ISRJ, but both have obvious limitations:
• Receiving end signal processing method: Relying on the time-frequency characteristics difference between the target and the interference, by constructing bandpass filters to segmentally filter out the interference, or through complex algorithms to estimate the interference parameters (such as duty cycle, sampling period), but the signal processing process is cumbersome, and real-time performance is limited.
• Transmitting end waveform design method: By designing special waveforms (such as sparse Doppler waveforms, intra-pulse orthogonal waveforms) to disrupt the continuity of the interference, but only focusing on the design at the transmitting end, still needing segmented processing at the receiving end to take effect, with low design freedom and poor compatibility.

Innovative solution: The "dual optimization" approach
The team broke through the traditional single design thinking, starting from "Transmitting end waveform + Receiver non-matched filtering" joint optimization, by establishing mathematical models, deriving analytical solutions, and iterative optimization, achieving dual improvements in anti-jamming performance and signal processing efficiency.

-  
Suppress the sidelobes of the transmitted signal pulse compression integral: Ensure the radar's detection accuracy of real targets, avoiding excessive sidelobes that mask the target signal.
-
